Project adaptations include WebP compression, resolution variants and isolation
of city lights by subtracting the blue channel from the night map's green channel.

Surface roughness regions and terrain normals by Solar System Scope, CC BY 4.0.
https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/download/8k_earth_specular_map.tif
https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/download/8k_earth_normal_map.tif
The relief-ocean maps carry tangent-space normal XY in the red and green channels
(amplified around the neutral value before compression), and the ocean mask
from the source specular map in blue. All three channels use linear data. The 8K cloud layer
retains the original cloud map's resolution.

European detail tiles (4096 x 3072 each), longitude -16 to 24 degrees,
latitude 33 to 63 degrees, EPSG:4326. Supplied via NASA GIBS, 500 m
tile matrix set, layers BlueMarble_NextGeneration and VIIRS_CityLights_2012.
https://gibs.earthdata.nasa.gov/wms/epsg4326/best/wms.cgi
europe-albedo.webp is a regional MODIS Blue Marble Next Generation composite
by Reto Stockli, NASA Earth Observatory. Broad-scale colors were matched to
the global albedo in linear light. Source and credit:
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/
europe-lights.webp isolates the bright and warm emission from the Earth at
Night 2012 composite. NASA Earth Observatory image by Robert Simmon, using
Suomi NPP VIIRS data provided courtesy of Chris Elvidge (NOAA National
Geophysical Data Center). https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/30028/
NASA media usage guidelines apply to both NASA images:
https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/

atmosphere.png is original numerical data generated for this scene by
web/scripts/build-earth-atmosphere.py. Its rows encode wavelength-dependent
transmittance and in-scattered radiance for the fixed solar elevation.
Background on the scattering model:
https://ebruneton.github.io/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering/
NASA astronaut photograph ISS070-E-1178 provided the visual reference for
the cool stratosphere above the warm, low-altitude sunrise:
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/sunrise-from-the-station-152259/

orbital-dawn.webp uses the supplied Home art. The exposed Earth and sky come from
the reference, with ImageGen reconstruction behind the original foreground and UI.
